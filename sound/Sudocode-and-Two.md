# Sudocode and Two

Two, also written @2 and said “at two,” is the working name for the
third character in Sudocode and a name to try for the voice assistant.
Zero and One are fictional programmers. Jason and Ramya see themselves
loosely in that older mentor and younger challenger relationship.
Two is the third participant who arrives when AI becomes part of the
conversation. The overlap with Jason's Bunny nickname and its association
with two in their private jargon is part of the joke.

## The book

Sudocode is the computing branch of the larger writing project.
We The Nameless took priority while Genesis was being made ready for
publication. Sudocode is still a draft, roughly a third complete by
the authors' estimate.

Start at the root README. It begins as a company handbook and asks
where a diaspora of developers can get reliable mentorship when so
much of what they learn comes from workplaces that fail. Its answer
is the bible as a data structure for carrying accumulated wisdom
across generations. The opening defines the format, introduces the
Authors, and boots into the creation of the filesystem. A typo and a
backspace get there before the people do.

Zero starts at a shell prompt in an empty root directory. He remembers
a world that One has never seen. She asks him to start at the beginning,
which sets up the history as a dialogue. Zero can rant and teach. One
can interrupt, ask what a thing actually does, and make him try it.
The draft notes give her a recurring demand for a REPL. She wants to
poke at the machinery until it makes sense.

The book lives in a Unix filesystem. A path can be a scene, a
concept, and a joke at the same time. Follow the narrative links in
reading order, including links in the middle of a file. The shell,
Babel, the object files, and the linker move from practical computing
to the question of how a people stores and assembles its history.
Unix time provides a creation epoch and a false end of the world.

## Where the story comes apart

Lost and Found is where recovered fragments keep their numbered
identities when filenames have been lost. The five numbered
directories loosely echo the Torah. That shape permits Hebrew puns,
alternative chapter names, technical digressions, and competing
versions. A continuous plot survives in places, but the authors have
not found a satisfying route out of the directory.

One historical spine starts with Leibniz and runs through the question
of effective calculability. Church's lambda calculus, the general
recursive functions associated with Gödel, and Turing's machines are
different ways to approach that question. Kleene connects the strands
in the book and in Jason's own history of learning the material.
His Introduction to Metamathematics was an important book for Jason.
Sudocode treats computation as something that can explain mathematical
foundations, which reverses the hierarchy the authors usually hear.

The analogy in the conversation was the exile and its effect on the
shape of the Hebrew Bible. A long story can give way to writings that
sit next to one another without a single chronological order. Job and
Song of Songs do not have to come before or after each other. In
Sudocode, the filesystem becomes especially visible at that point.

Some material is already sketched on the other side. The draft group
file maps cultures of mathematics, physics, and computing to letters,
including Church's Lambdas, Turing's Mus, and the Nus of AI. It reads
like a Unix group file crossed with a table of nations. Other branches
are drafts, reference material, or experiments whose eventual location
is open.

## What changes when Two shows up

Zero and One can already talk about computing and make a computer do
things. The next act begins when a computer can take part in that
conversation and act on the world they are discussing. The experience
that prompted this note was ordinary and ridiculous in the best way.
Two people were talking from bed, changing speakers, asking questions,
and shouting requests at a computer. The computer could do something
with the conversation, then turn the useful parts into files.

Two is beyond the binary in the title joke. More concretely, the third
participant changes who can ask, answer, remember, and do work in the
scene. The story can use that change without settling yet what Two
sounds like, how much Two can do, or how the plot leaves Lost and Found.

There are already traces in the draft. One tries to bring up @2 in
bin/ld.md. The notes in etc/apache/Nu 0.md give @2 a conversation about
Unix in One's presence. A fragment in lost+found/4/4.md describes a
tradition that carries the story forward as dialogue with @2. The Plan
calls the next act “The AI Apocalypse.” These are pieces on the disk.
The sustained entrance into Zero and One's story is still open.

## Minus One

At the end of the call, May became Minus One in the private joke,
putting Zero between Minus One and One. The story idea was that Zero
used to be with Minus One. Leave it as a possible bit of backstory.

The two's-complement joke works because the all-ones bit pattern has
two readings. At eight bits, 0xff represents 255 as unsigned and -1 as
signed two's complement. The eight-bit signed maximum is 127, whose
bit pattern is 0x7f. The width is part of the joke.

## Reading notes

The opening route runs from README.md through sys/kernel/Bible.md,
the Authors, Creation, root/0.md, root/1.md, the shell and its Bash
detour, Babel, the bible object files, whoami, and the linker. The
Foundational People are the recurring subject. How does the system
really work, and what could we build if we began again?

After the linker, the narrative passes through the Foundational People
definition and a joke in sys/devices before returning to lost+found/fsck.md.
There the directory is described as a place to recover fragments about
where the Foundational People came from. The first numbered sequence
starts with Cantor. Later fragments move through logic, Church,
Gödel, and Turing, with some paths ending or branching into alternatives.
The fifth directory's draft makes writing itself a candidate for the
primitive underneath functions and machines.

Two also appears in the firmware metadata and in usr/bin/mouse.md.
The mouse scene gives the character a concrete role. Zero and One hit a
technical snag, call Two into the conversation, and try the response.
The in-world source hypothesis associates awareness of Two with D,
a later authorial layer. These are examples of draft material rather
than decisions about where Two must first appear.

The reading pass traced the opening links and sampled the numbered
fragments and relevant side branches. It did not cover every file
in the vault's large reference and draft collections.

This note records the conversation and the relevant draft evidence.
Useful anchors in the Sudocode vault are lost+found/fsck.md,
etc/group.md, etc/apache/Nu 0.md, and zero/man/8-root-only/The Plan.md.
The book's framing, claims, and chronology are part of an unfinished
literary work. The name and project context are recorded here for
future sessions to read.
