	\begin{together}[8][5000]
	\Def{The Yahwist}[J]{%
	The name traditionally given to the author or source whose stories
	characteristically use the divine name \textsc{Yhwh} from the beginning.
	J is associated with vivid, earthy narratives, anthropomorphic portrayals
	of God, and stories of family, sex, deception, and other timeless,
	universal human themes. Unlike the priestly sources, J places little
	emphasis on law, ritual, or moralizing, preferring to explore human nature
	through narrative. The J source also develops female characters more deeply
	than the other sources and appears not to have been a priest, but rather
	to have had some connection or affinity to the royal court of the Davidic
	monarchy.

	J is commonly dated to around 922~B.C., making J the first and earliest
	author of the bible, apart from a handful of brief poems and songs.
	J is credited with writing most of the bible's best-known stories,
	including the Garden of Eden, Cain and Abel, Noah's Flood (with P),
	the Tower of Babel, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (with E and P), Joseph
	and his brothers (with E), baby Moses in the basket, the burning bush
	(with E), narrative sections of Numbers, and the death of Moses (with E, P, and D).
	Recent source analysis has shown J to have been additionally the probable
	author of much of Joshua, Judges, 1 Samuel, all of 2 Samuel, and the first
	two chapters of Kings.

	Some scholars have argued that J was a woman.
	Some have argued J was not, in fact, a religious author,
	and that J's writing reflects one of the most subtle and well developed
	senses of humor in world literature, one rooted in irony, wordplay,
	and understatement that readers have been misunderstanding for
	nearly three thousand years.

	No author in history has had a greater influence on world culture,
	religion, and literature, nor is any other author a close second.
	}
	%
	\phantom{Praise the \textsc{Lord}!} J's back.
	\end{together}
