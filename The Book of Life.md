
Universe. (noun)

A type of product or project where the creators give the users so much power to do their own thing that the creators end up having basically zero ability to affect anything, and often even lose the ability to know a large percent of what the users do \[with / in / on\] their creation.

Examples of creators (upstairs) and the universes they've made (downstairs) are:
- Linus: The Linux kernel
- Guido: Python
- Roger Dingledine: Tor
- Ilya Sutskever (et al): ChatGPT
- Some Nameless Humans at microsoft: That editor everyone used to use to write code, back when we used to write code. Also the editor everyone still uses to write code, because we still do that. That's not two editors. Just one. It's called `code`.
- Some Nameless Humans at obsidian: Obsidian. It's basically the same as the previous one, but better, now that English is executable.

This concept of Universe should not be confused with the very different sense of the term "universe" (lowercase) like the following:

- JK Rowling: The universe of Harry Potter
- Ayn Rand: The universe of Atlas Shrugged.
- Hitchcock: All those movies.
- Some Nameless Humans at nintendo (It was five Japanese guys): Super Mario.

Note: "Nameless" doesn't mean we don't know their names. A creator is Nameless when their name is not publicly or visibly associated with a universe or Universe they created either (a) by themselves, or (b) in collaboration with some other creators they're friends with, or (c) as one creator forming a part of a large pantheon, also known in some circles as a "company."

What makes a product or a project a Universe (as opposed to a universe) is that the creator gives those inside their creation so much freedom that the creator effectively loses the ability to make their users do anything, and thus effectively automates themselves out of existence. Though not completely. Cuz they still have to work on making the thing. Until they quit.
